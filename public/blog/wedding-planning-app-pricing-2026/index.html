How Much Should a Wedding Planning App Actually Cost?

Pricing App By Weddings.io Editorial · June 23, 2026 · ~2,400 words

The honest breakdown: what wedding planning apps cost to run, what you should pay, and why Weddings.io keeps every tool free while charging $9.99/month for the stuff that actually needs a server.

The average couple spends $35,000–$60,000 on their wedding. In that context, paying $14.99/month for a planning app seems trivial. Wedding app companies know this. Which is why The Knot charges $14.99/month, Zola charges $12.99/month, and Joy charges $9.99/month — for tools that are mostly free on competitors' platforms.

The question couples never ask is: what does it actually cost to run a wedding planning app? And once you know the answer, the pricing of most platforms looks very different.

The Core Question

"If a budget calculator is just math running in your browser, why are you paying $15/month for it? And if you are paying $15/month, what are you actually getting?"

What a Wedding Planning App Actually Does

Most wedding planning tools fall into two categories: things that run in your browser and things that need a server. The distinction matters because browser-based tools cost essentially nothing to provide — and server-based features have real costs that justify a subscription.

Browser-Based Tools (Cost: $0 to run)

  • Budget calculator — arithmetic. Runs entirely in JavaScript.
  • Checklist builder — a list. No server required.
  • Guest list manager — a table. Saves to your browser's localStorage.
  • Vendor CRM — contact tracking. Browser storage.
  • Ceremony timeline builder — date math. No backend needed.
  • Floor planner 2D/3D — geometry rendered in WebGL. Pure browser.
  • Culture-specific tools — logic and checklists. Zero server cost.

Every one of these tools can run completely free, permanently, with no account required. Charging a monthly subscription for them is charging you for math your own computer is doing.

Server-Based Features (Cost: real, but small)

  • Cloud sync — saves your data to a database so you can access it from any device. Costs ~$0.05/month per active user at current database pricing.
  • Photo storage — holding your wedding inspiration, vendor shots, and venue images. Costs ~$0.02/GB/month.
  • Cross-device access — requires an account and a backend. Minimal but real cost.
  • AI visualizer renders — each render calls Gemini AI. Costs ~$0.50–$1.50 per render at API pricing.
  • PDF export — server-side generation. Negligible cost.
  • Multi-wedding dashboard — for planners managing multiple clients. Database queries per session.

Why We Can Keep the Tools Free

The honest answer: vendors pay for the platform, not couples.

Every vendor on Weddings.io pays a territory lock fee — $10 per 100,000 people in their market, per month. A wedding photographer in Surrey BC pays $24/month to be the exclusive featured photographer in that city. A decorator in London UK pays $127/month. That recurring vendor revenue funds the infrastructure so couples never have to pay to use the planning tools.

This is structurally different from The Knot or WeddingWire, where vendor fees fund the business but couple subscriptions are sold as an additional revenue layer on top. At Weddings.io, couple subscriptions exist only for features that genuinely require server infrastructure — cloud sync, photo storage, cross-device access. Everything else stays free because it costs nothing to provide.

The Model in One Sentence

"Vendors pay to be found. Couples plan for free. The server-side features cost $9.99/month because servers cost money. That's the whole model."
