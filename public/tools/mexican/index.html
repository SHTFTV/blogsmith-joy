💒 Ceremony Traditions Guide

The four key Mexican wedding ceremony elements — each with its Padrinos, logistics, and timing notes.

El Lazo (The Lasso)

🪢 Lazo de Matrimonio

A large rosary or decorative cord placed in a figure-eight around the couple's shoulders, symbolising eternal union and infinity.

  • Placed by Padrinos de Lazo — must be a married couple
  • Duration: 15–20 minutes during ceremony
  • Size: typically 25–30 feet long, 3-inch beads or rope
  • Removed after ceremony by Padrinos
  • Cost: $80–$200 for ornate rosary lasso

Las Arras (The Coins)

💰 Las Arras Matrimoniales

13 gold coins (arras) presented by groom to bride in a decorative box or tray, symbolising prosperity and financial trust.

  • 13 coins = Christ + 12 Apostles (Catholic tradition)
  • Coins may be gold-plated or ceremonial coin set
  • Placed on tray by Padrinos de Arras
  • Bride pours coins back into groom's hands
  • Cost: $40–$150 for decorative coin set

El Ramo (The Bouquet)

💐 Ramo de la Novia

Bridal bouquet typically sponsored by the Madrina de Ramo. Traditional Mexican bouquets feature vibrant colours — cempasúchil (marigolds), roses, birds of paradise.

  • Sponsored by Madrina de Ramo
  • Toss bouquet at reception for next to marry
  • Some brides keep and preserve the bouquet
  • Cost: $150–$500 depending on size and florals

El Brindis (The Toast)

🥂 Brindis Nupcial

The wedding toast — typically with Mexican sparkling wine or tequila. Padrino de Bebidas sponsors all beverages including the toast bottle.

  • Cava or sparkling wine for formal toast
  • Tequila for traditional family shots
  • Mariachi typically plays during brindis
  • El Grito (cheer) led by the MC
