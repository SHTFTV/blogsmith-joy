🙏 Southeast Asian Wedding Planner

งานแต่งงาน · Đám cưới · Kasal · ពិធីមង្គលការ · Pernikahan

Buddhist monk scheduling, multiple outfit change timeline (Thai brides average 3–4 changes), water blessing logistics, ritual prop checklist, and traditional dance planner. For Thai, Vietnamese, Filipino, Cambodian, and Indonesian weddings.

👘 Outfit Change Timeline Builder

Thai and Vietnamese brides typically change 3–4 times on their wedding day. Each change takes 20–40 minutes. Build your timeline around these transitions — they affect every other vendor's schedule.

🙏 Buddhist Monk Scheduler

Monk blessings are the centrepiece of Thai and Cambodian wedding mornings. Monks must leave before noon (Buddhist rule). Plan their arrival, offerings, and departure with precision.

⚠️ Key Rule: Buddhist monks must complete their meal and leave the venue before 12:00 noon. Plan everything backwards from this hard deadline.
6:00 AM — 6:30 AM
Monks Arrive
Typically 5–9 monks depending on family tradition. A senior monk (phra) leads the group. Designated entrance — not through main guest entrance. Shoes left outside.
Require: Designated seating area facing East · Floor cushions or elevated seats for monks
6:30 AM — 7:30 AM
Merit-Making Ceremony
Couple presents alms to monks — rice, food offerings, incense, flowers. Monks chant sutras for 30–60 minutes. Guests kneel in respect during chanting.
Require: Alms bowl provisions per monk · Incense · White jasmine garlands
7:30 AM — 8:30 AM
Monk Blessing & Sacred Thread
Sai Sin (sacred white thread) is connected from Buddha image through monks' hands to the couple. Monks chant while holding the thread — blessing transfers through the thread.
Require: Sai Sin thread (white cotton, 50m+) · Buddha image on altar · Candles and incense
8:30 AM — 10:00 AM
Monks' Meal
Monks must eat before noon. Food is prepared separately from wedding catering — strictly vegetarian (or Buddhist diet). Monks eat in silence, seated on elevated area.
Require: Separate dining area · Vegetarian Thai meal · Serving by family members (not caterers)
10:00 AM — 10:30 AM
Monks' Departure
Family thanks monks with sealed envelope donations (in white envelopes — never red). Monks depart with alms received. No applause — bow respectfully.
Require: White envelopes per monk · Transportation confirmed · Family member to escort out
10:30 AM onwards
Wedding Ceremony Begins
After monks depart, couple changes to Western/formal outfit and proceeds to main ceremony. Rod Nam Sang (water blessing) typically follows.
Buffer: 30 min between monks leaving and ceremony starting — for reset and outfit change
💧 Water Blessing Logistics (Rod Nam Sang)

The Rod Nam Sang is a post-ceremony ritual where elders pour blessed water over the couple's joined hands. Plan who participates, the order, and the props needed.

0 of 8 steps prepared
1
Sacred Water Prepared (Nam Mon)
Monks or senior elder blesses water the morning of the wedding — white jasmine, gold leaf, holy water mixed
2
Conch Shell Sourced & Cleaned
The conch (Sang) is the vessel used to pour water — must be large, clean, and ceremonially prepared
3
Wedding Bench Set (Mon Bench)
Elevated wooden or ornate bench for couple to sit — hands joined over ceremony bowl in front
4
Elder Participation List Confirmed
Order confirmed: groom's parents first, then bride's parents, then grandparents, then other elders by seniority
5
Gold Mong Kol (Crown) Ready
Traditional Thai sacred crown — twin crowns connected by string, placed on couple's heads before water blessing
6
Photographer Briefed on Water Blessing Order
Photographer must be positioned before elders approach — this is one of the most important photo moments
7
Hand Towels Ready for Couple
Soft white towels for couple to dry hands after blessing — should be embroidered or decorative for photos
8
MC Briefed on Blessing Protocol
MC must announce the start of Rod Nam Sang and instruct guests to remain seated and silent during the blessing
