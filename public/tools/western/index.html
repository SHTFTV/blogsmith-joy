⛪ Ceremony Type Guide

Timing, music, and logistics differ significantly by ceremony type. Select your format to see the standard run-of-day.

Protestant Church — Standard Timeline (~40 min)

–30 min
Guests SeatedString quartet or organist plays prelude music as guests arrive. Ushers seat bride's family on left, groom's on right.
–10 min
Mothers SeatedGroom's mother escorted in first, then bride's mother. Marks official start of ceremony sequence.
0:00
ProcessionalGroomsmen, then bridesmaids, then flower girl/ring bearer, then bride with father. Music: Canon in D, Trumpet Voluntary, or chosen processional piece.
+5 min
Opening & WelcomeOfficiant welcomes guests. Declaration of intent ("Who gives this woman…").
+10 min
Readings1–2 readings. Scripture or secular poetry depending on tradition. Reader approaches podium.
+18 min
Vows & Ring ExchangeOfficiant leads vows — traditional, personal, or mixed. Ring exchange follows immediately.
+28 min
Unity Ceremony (optional)Unity candle, sand ceremony, or wine ceremony. 3–5 minutes.
+33 min
Pronouncement & First KissOfficiant pronounces couple married. First kiss. Guests stand and applaud.
+35 min
RecessionalCouple exits to recessional music (Mendelssohn Wedding March, Ode to Joy). Wedding party follows. Guests exit row by row.

Catholic Nuptial Mass — Full Communion (~75 min)

–20 min
Guests Seated, Prelude MusicChurch organist or string quartet plays as guests arrive.
0:00
ProcessionalPriest leads procession. Altar servers, then wedding party, then bride. Music selected must be approved by the parish.
+10 min
Liturgy of the WordTwo Scripture readings, responsorial psalm, Gospel reading, homily. This alone runs 20–25 minutes.
+35 min
Rite of MarriageExchange of consent, vows, blessing of rings, ring exchange. Formal declaration by priest.
+50 min
Liturgy of the Eucharist (Communion)Only Catholic guests in good standing receive Communion. Non-Catholic guests remain seated. 15–20 minutes.
+68 min
Final Blessing & DismissalPriest blesses the couple and congregation. Recessional.

⚠️ Music selection must be approved by the parish music director. Popular songs (e.g. Ave Maria, Panis Angelicus) are typically permitted. Secular songs are not. Confirm at least 6 weeks prior.

Civil / Registry Office Ceremony (~25 min)

0:00
Couple & Witnesses ArriveCivil ceremony requires 2 witnesses minimum (not related to each other in most jurisdictions). Registrar checks ID documents.
+5 min
Opening DeclarationRegistrar states the legal words of marriage. These are fixed by law and cannot be changed.
+10 min
Personal Vows (optional)Civil ceremonies allow personal vows as long as they contain no religious content.
+15 min
Ring Exchange & SigningRings exchanged. Marriage register signed by couple, 2 witnesses, and registrar.
+22 min
Pronouncement & CertificateCouple pronounced married. Marriage certificate issued immediately.

💡 Most couples pair a simple civil ceremony with a larger reception or a symbolic ceremony with a celebrant. Book the civil ceremony first — dates fill 3–6 months ahead in most cities.

Contemporary / Garden Ceremony (~45 min)

–25 min
Guests Arrive, PreludeLive musician or curated playlist. Guests seat themselves — no formal ushering in many contemporary ceremonies.
0:00
ProcessionalWedding party enters to couple's chosen music. Bride enters last — with or without escort per couple's preference.
+5 min
Welcome & Celebrant IntroductionCelebrant sets the tone — can be warm and personal. No fixed liturgical requirements.
+10 min
Readings & Tributes1–3 readings. Friends or family members. Secular poetry, prose, song lyrics, or personal writing.
+22 min
VowsFully personalised. Couple typically writes their own. 1–3 minutes each.
+30 min
Unity Ritual (optional)Sand, wine, candle, tree planting, ring warming (rings passed through crowd), etc.
+38 min
Pronouncement, Kiss, RecessionalCouple's chosen song for exit. Often a joyful, upbeat track rather than classical.

Fusion / Interfaith Ceremony (~50 min)

A fusion ceremony combines elements from two or more traditions. Common combinations: Christian + South Asian, Catholic + Jewish, Protestant + Hindu. The structural challenge is integrating rituals that have different spatial and timing requirements.

0:00
Processional — CombinedAgree on one processional order. Groom's cultural tradition often leads.
+8 min
Opening — Both Officiants WelcomeTwo officiants (or one bilingual celebrant). Each tradition welcomed equally in the opening.
+15 min
Tradition 1 RitualE.g. ring warming, unity candle, scripture reading from one faith.
+25 min
Tradition 2 RitualE.g. garland exchange, Lasso ceremony, Chuppah blessing — from second culture.
+35 min
Shared VowsWritten to honour both traditions. Both officiants co-lead the exchange.
+44 min
Pronouncement & RecessionalDual pronouncement honouring both families. Exit music chosen to feel inclusive of both cultures.

💡 Rehearsal is non-negotiable for fusion ceremonies. Book a 90-minute rehearsal the day before — two traditions, two officiants, multiple rituals. Nothing should be unrehearsed.

🎻 String Quartet Event Scheduler

A string quartet serves different roles at different moments. This is the correct deployment — not background music all evening, but deliberate placement at the moments that matter.

Prelude — Guest Arrival
30–45 min before ceremony
Quartet plays as guests are seated. Sets emotional tone before the ceremony begins. Background level — conversation should be possible.
Repertoire: Bach Air on G String, Vivaldi Spring, Debussy Clair de Lune arr., Satie Gymnopédie
Processional — Wedding Party
Ceremony open
Wedding party walks in. Tempo is steady — 60–70 BPM. Quartet must be able to extend or loop if wedding party walks slowly.
Canon in D, Clarke's Trumpet Voluntary, Bach Jesu Joy of Man's Desiring
Bride's Processional
Ceremony — key moment
The most significant musical moment. Must start exactly when the bride appears at the entrance — brief the quartet on the cue signal (typically a nod from the coordinator).
Wagner Bridal Chorus, Canon in D, Pachelbel, or custom piece
Signing / Interlude
During register signing
1–3 minutes of music while the register is signed. Guests watch — something beautiful and unhurried. The quartet can play something slightly more contemporary here.
A Thousand Years (arr.), Turning Page, Can't Help Falling in Love (arr.)
Recessional
Ceremony close
Joyful, energetic exit music. Tempo increases significantly — 90–120 BPM. This is a celebration, not a march.
Mendelssohn Wedding March, Beethoven Ode to Joy, Signed Sealed Delivered (arr.)
Cocktail Hour
60–90 min
Strolling or stationed quartet. Background level. Mix of classical and contemporary arrangements. Guests should be able to converse easily.
Popular songs arranged for quartet, light classical, film scores. No amplification needed.
💡 Booking notes 2026: A 4-piece string quartet in Vancouver, Toronto, or London costs $800–$2,200 for ceremony + cocktail hour (3–4 hours). Book 4–6 months ahead for peak season Saturdays. Always request: do they have a standard wedding repertoire list, and will they learn a specific piece if needed (usually yes, for an additional fee)?
🗣️ Speech Order Planner

Track who is speaking, in what order, for how long, and whether they've confirmed. The standard order — but customise to your event.

0 of 4 speakers confirmed
1
Father of the Bride (or Family Representative)
Welcomes guests on behalf of the family. Shares memories of the bride. Formally welcomes the groom into the family. Proposes the first toast to the couple.
⏱ Target: 4–6 minutes · Tone: Warm, emotional, celebratory
☐
2
Best Man
Thanks bridesmaids on behalf of the groom. Shares (appropriate) stories about the groom. Acknowledges how the bride has changed him. Proposes a toast to the couple.
⏱ Target: 5–7 minutes · Tone: Humorous, warm, not inappropriate
☐
3
Groom
Thanks both families, the wedding party, and guests. Thanks the best man and groomsmen. Expresses love and gratitude to the bride. Proposes a toast to the bridesmaids and to the bride.
⏱ Target: 4–6 minutes · Tone: Heartfelt, grateful, personal
☐
4
Bride (optional — increasingly standard)
Increasingly common to have the bride speak. Thanks families and wedding party from her perspective. Adds personal stories. Can be more intimate and emotional.
⏱ Target: 3–5 minutes · Tone: Personal, emotional, as she chooses
☐
💡 Modern practice: move all speeches to before dinner (after starter is cleared). Guests are engaged and sober. Speeches after main course mean half the room is tired. Total speech time budget: 20–28 minutes for 4 speakers.
