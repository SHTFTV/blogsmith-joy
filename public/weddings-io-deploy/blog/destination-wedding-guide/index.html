Planning a South Asian Destination Wedding

12 min read • Updated January 2026

Dreaming of saying your vows on a Bali clifftop, a Tuscan vineyard, or a Caribbean beach? Destination weddings have become increasingly popular for South Asian couples looking to create intimate, unforgettable celebrations.

But taking a multi-day South Asian wedding abroad comes with unique challenges. Here's everything you need to know.

Top Destinations for South Asian Weddings

🏝️ Bali, Indonesia

Why it works: Hindu culture, stunning temples, luxury villas, and incredible value. Balinese vendors understand Indian ceremonies. Budget: $$$. Best for: 50-200 guests.

🇮🇹 Italy (Tuscany, Amalfi, Lake Como)

Why it works: Romantic scenery, world-class food and wine, historic villas. Budget: $$$$. Best for: 100-300 guests. Note: Civil ceremony required for legal marriage.

🇲🇽 Mexico (Cancun, Los Cabos, Riviera Maya)

Why it works: Direct flights from North America, all-inclusive resorts, beautiful beaches. Budget: $$$. Best for: 100-400 guests.

🇹🇭 Thailand (Phuket, Koh Samui, Chiang Mai)

Why it works: Exotic yet affordable, excellent service culture, beautiful resorts. Budget: $$. Best for: 50-200 guests.

🏝️ Maldives

Why it works: Ultimate luxury, overwater villas, crystal-clear waters. Budget: $$$$$. Best for: Intimate weddings under 50 guests.

Key Considerations

1. Legal Requirements

Every country has different rules for legal marriages:

  • Some countries (Italy, France) require a civil ceremony
  • Others (Mexico, Bali) allow religious ceremonies to be legally binding
  • Many couples do a legal ceremony at home and a symbolic destination celebration
  • Research visa requirements for you, your officiant, and guests

2. Guest Count Reality Check

Destination weddings naturally filter your guest list. Expect 30-50% of your invite list to actually attend. This can be a blessing - smaller, more intimate celebrations with people who truly matter.

3. Vendor Logistics

  • Local vendors: Usually more affordable, understand local logistics
  • Flying in vendors: Your trusted photographer/decorator, but add flights + accommodation
  • Hybrid approach: Local coordination + key vendors from home

4. South Asian-Specific Needs

Not all destinations easily accommodate:

  • Havan/fire ceremonies (some venues prohibit open flames)
  • Baraat with dhol and horse
  • Vegetarian/Jain/Halal catering for large groups
  • Finding a pandit, granthi, or imam
  • Traditional decor and mandap setup

Research thoroughly and ask venues about their experience with Indian weddings.

Budget Planning

Destination wedding budgets work differently:

  • You'll save on: Guest count (fewer people = less catering), potentially venue costs in affordable destinations
  • You'll spend more on: Travel, accommodation, shipping decor/outfits, vendor travel, multi-day events
  • Guest expectations: Consider covering some costs for immediate family, offering group hotel rates

Timeline

Destination weddings require more lead time:

  • 18-24 months before: Choose destination, visit venues, book date
  • 12-18 months: Send save-the-dates (guests need time to plan)
  • 9-12 months: Book vendors, finalize guest accommodations
  • 6 months: Send formal invitations
  • 3 months: Final guest count, detail planning
  • 1 month: Ship decor, confirm all logistics
  • 1 week: Arrive early to handle setup and last-minute issues

Making It Work

The key to a successful destination South Asian wedding:

  • Hire a local planner who has done Indian weddings before
  • Visit in person before booking anything major
  • Have backup plans for weather, vendor issues, travel delays
  • Communicate clearly with guests about expectations and costs
  • Embrace flexibility - some traditions may need adapting

Destination weddings take more planning, but the result is a truly unique celebration that your guests will remember forever.
